Overview

Kalsubai Peak is the highest point in Maharashtra, soaring to a majestic altitude of 1,646 metres (5,400 feet) in the Sahyadri range of Western Ghats. Situated within the Kalsubai Harishchandragad Wildlife Sanctuary in the Ahilyanagar district, it is affectionately celebrated as the 'Everest of Maharashtra'.

The peak provides an awe-inspiring 360-degree panoramic vantage point, with sightlines extending to historic fortresses such as Alang, Madan, Kulang (AMK), Ratangad, Harishchandragad, and the sparkling emerald waters of Arthur Lake in Bhandardara. At the very summit rests the sacred shrine of Kalsubai Devi, drawing pilgrims and thousands of adventure enthusiasts every weekend.

Historical Highlights

The Highest Summit of Maharashtra

Kalsubai marks the highest geographic altitude in the entire Western Ghats of Maharashtra, acting as a natural weather divide between the Deccan Plateau and Konkan.

The Legend of Kalsubai Devi

Local folklore tells of a tribal housemaid named Kalsubai who sought refuge from unjust masters in the high mountain meadows and disappeared into the summit rock, blessing the valley.

Famous Steel Ladders of Kalsubai

To make the sheer vertical rock bands accessible, the Forest Department and local villagers installed 4 sturdy steel ladders, creating a thrilling and safe climbing experience.

Attraction Description
Kalsubai Devi Temple at Summit Small traditional red shrine honoring the goddess, surrounded by prayer bells and colorful flags.
Thrilling Steel Ladder Sections Four vertical iron ladder climbs fixed securely to rocky cliff faces along the ridge.
AMK (Alang, Madan, Kulang) Vistas Direct sightline to the legendary trio of impregnable rock-tower fortresses.
Wilson Dam & Arthur Lake Views Gleaming turquoise water bodies visible in the Bhandardara valley below.
Ocean of Clouds (Sunrise Phenomenon) Witness the sun rise over dense white cloud inversions blanketed below the summit.
Kalsubai Wildlife Sanctuary Lush mountain flora, medicinal plants, butterfly swarms, and endangered Sahyadri bird species.
