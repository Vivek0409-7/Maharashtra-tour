Overview

Sinhagad Fort (originally known as Kondhana) is one of the most celebrated and historic hill fortresses in Maharashtra. Perched majestically atop an isolated cliff in the Bhuleshwar range of the Sahyadri mountains, approximately 30 kilometres southwest of Pune, it stands at an elevation of 1,312 metres above sea level.

The fort is renowned for the epic Battle of Sinhagad in 1670, where the Maratha warrior Subedar Tanaji Malusare scaled the sheer vertical cliffs using a monitor lizard (Ghorpad) and recaptured the fortress from the Mughal garrison. Upon hearing of Tanaji's supreme sacrifice, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj famously uttered the immortal words: 'Gad aala, pan sinh gela' (The fort is captured, but the lion is lost). Sinhagad continues to be a vibrant center of Maratha pride and a cherished destination for trekkers and history enthusiasts.

Historical Highlights

The Legendary Battle of 1670

In 1670, Tanaji Malusare led a daring midnight assault scaling the precipitous Kondhana cliffs with his mavlas. Tanaji fought fiercely and vanquished the Mughal commandant Udaybhan Rathod, securing a glorious victory for Swarajya at the cost of his own life.

Ancient Strategic Roots

Carvings in the Kaundinya Eshwar temple indicate the fort was built over 2,000 years ago. The citadel passed through the hands of the Devgiri Yadavas, Bahmanis, and Adilshahi before Shivaji Maharaj seized it in 1647 to establish Maratha hegemony over Pune.

Freedom Struggle Legacy

During India's freedom movement, the nationalist leader Lokmanya Bal Gangadhar Tilak maintained a summer bungalow on Sinhagad Fort, where historic discussions with Mahatma Gandhi and national thinkers took place.

Major Attractions

Attraction Description
Tanaji Malusare Memorial & Samadhi The sacred memorial dedicated to Veer Tanaji Malusare honoring his supreme sacrifice.
Pune Darwaza & Kalyan Darwaza Monumental historical gateway arches exhibiting robust Maratha military architecture.
Kondhaneshwar Ancient Shiva Temple Historic centuries-old stone shrine dedicated to Lord Shiva situated inside the citadel.
Tanaji Kada (Precipitous Cliff) The sheer vertical rock cliff scaled by Tanaji and his warriors during the 1670 night raid.
Amruteshwar Tank & Devtake Natural sweet groundwater cisterns providing pure mountain spring water all year.
Wind Point & Kade Lot Point Spectacular vantage points offering refreshing gales and panoramic views across Khadakwasla Dam.
